# Product depth backlog

Last updated: 2026-08-08

A file-anchored audit of every product area against the product-depth wishlist, produced by
twelve independent auditors reading the working tree. Each gap states the user-visible symptom,
where the fix lands, whether it needs a migration or an Edge Function change, and how much a
paying Brazilian family would care.

The audit was taken against the tree as of 2026-08-08. The section below records what has since
been closed; everything else in this document is still open.

---

## Closed since the audit

- **Assignment was a free-text display name.** `tasks.owner_member_id` and
  `shopping_items.owner_member_id` had been FKs to `family_members` since the initial schema and
  were always NULL, so a member who renamed themselves became two people in the workload split
  and their old items could never be re-attributed; two members sharing a first name merged into
  one bucket; and a removed member's name stayed stamped on every item they ever owned. The
  pointer is now the truth and `owner_label` a display cache kept in step by triggers — one
  syncing the label when the pointer moves, one propagating a rename, and a reassign-by-name
  branch so a label-only write (Nina's proposals, older clients) still works. Ambiguity resolves
  to NULL rather than guessing: attributing work to the wrong person is worse than leaving it
  unattributed. "Casa" stays unattributable, and a member literally named Casa cannot absorb the
  house bucket. `private.nina_weekly_metrics` and `get_workload_summary` both group by member id,
  so the Sunday insight and Nina's own answer finally agree with the card. Backfill runs with the
  `updated_at`/version triggers suspended — otherwise touching every historical row would have
  made the next insight report every finished task as completed that week.
  `member_management.test.sql` 29 → 47, plus 12 Deno tests and 13 new iOS tests.

- **AI consent was device-local, so revoking it did nothing.** An adult who tapped "Revogar
  consentimento" flipped a row on that iPhone only: their partner kept chatting, and the next
  Sunday `get_nina_weekly_candidates()` still selected the household and shipped
  `open_tasks_by_owner` — a map of member display names to open-task counts — to `gpt-5.5`.
  Reinstalling silently reset consent to "never accepted" with no record it had ever been granted
  or withdrawn. Consent is the LGPD legal basis for that processing, so this was a compliance
  defect rather than a UX one. `public.nina_ai_consents` now records one live grant per adult per
  home and *keeps* withdrawn rows, because LGPD expects demonstrable consent; `record_nina_ai_consent`
  is adults-only; and both `begin_nina_chat_run` and `get_nina_weekly_candidates` require a live
  grant, so a direct RPC call cannot bypass it — the same double-check as the adult gate. The
  client now writes through the RPC and treats the home context as the source of truth, and a
  failed write restores what is actually true on the server instead of silently flipping the
  switch. `nina_ai_v2.test.sql` 54 → 76, `rls_policies.test.sql` 44 → 45 (25 tables).

- **A single failed sync cost a subscriber their features.** `refreshBackendStatus` adopted the
  server's answer unconditionally, and `get_current_premium_status()` returns an identical payload
  for "no row" and "row says inactive" — so a subscriber whose one sync call 503'd, or who was
  offline at purchase time, was downgraded and shown a red error while StoreKit still held a
  valid transaction. The repair targets the *server*, not the label: because the gates read the
  household entitlement out of `premium_subscriptions`, an optimistic client flag would have
  fixed the copy and changed nothing real. A verified, unrevoked, unexpired transaction for a
  configured product plus an inactive server answer now re-POSTs the JWS to create the row;
  `isActive` stays false until the server agrees, and the surface says the purchase is being
  registered rather than claiming premium or reporting failure. A last-synced-transaction ledger
  in protected storage bounds the retry — and removes the two upserts the app had been POSTing on
  *every* foreground for every subscriber.
- **AI budget reservations stranded across a month boundary.** Reservations were taken against
  `current_month_start()` at reservation time and released against it again at completion time, so
  a run begun at 23:59:59 on the last day of a month released against the next month's row —
  which may not exist — leaving the reservation stranded and permanently shrinking that month's
  headroom against a hard US$20 cap. `nina_ai_runs.budget_month_start` now records the month the
  run charged, every settle path uses it, and `current_month_start()` survives only on the two
  reservation paths. `nina_ai_v2.test.sql` 46 → 54, pinned by moving a run's stored month rather
  than the clock.
- **Premium gated nothing.** All three agreed gates are now enforced where the resource is
  spent, not in the client. `get_nina_weekly_candidates()` requires a covered household **and**
  `families.weekly_digest_enabled` — the digest had been generating for up to 25 families a night
  against the US$5/month insights cap with no entitlement check and no opt-out. Attachments raise
  `P0001 nina_attachments_require_premium` inside `begin_nina_chat_run`, before the idempotency
  lookup (so a retry is refused too) and before moderation (so a refusal costs no OpenAI spend);
  `nina-chat` only translates it to `403 attachments_require_premium`. The per-user chat claim is
  tiered 30/hour covered vs 10/day free, with the per-family 100/day claim unchanged. The dead
  `nina.weeklyDigestEnabled` `@AppStorage` key now writes a real household setting through the
  serialized mutation queue, owner/admin only; the equally dead `nina.smartSuggestionsEnabled`
  row was deleted rather than left as a switch that visibly does nothing. `premium.test.sql`
  55 → 78, pinning each gate from both sides. **Still open in this area:** the local-vs-server
  entitlement downgrade (a subscriber whose sync failed once is locked out the moment gates
  exist — now that they do, this is no longer cosmetic and is the next premium item).
- **Premium was per-payer, and the paywall sold what the app could not deliver.**
  `premium_subscriptions` now carries a `family_id` that only the database decides — a
  `BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE` trigger derives it from the payer's active family and only while
  `is_family_member` holds, so no client or Edge Function can assert coverage of a house, and
  the entitlement releases itself when a payer leaves. `get_current_home_context()` gained a
  `premium` block carrying exactly `is_active`, `status`, `expires_at`, and `AppStore.householdPremium`
  is what every future gate reads. The SELECT policy was deliberately *not* broadened to family
  members: the row holds `signed_transaction_info` and `app_account_token`, and the latter is the
  payer's `auth.uid()`, so a row-scoped policy cannot say "the house is covered" without also
  disclosing who paid. On the paywall, "Rotinas inteligentes" and "Divisão mais justa" were
  deleted — one is discarded for everyone while `NINA_AI_V2_ENABLED = NO`, the other was the
  fabricated chart — and subscription title/length/price-per-period, Terms and Privacy links, and
  a native manage-subscription sheet were added. `web/src/pages/termos.astro` and
  `Nina/Nina.storekit` did not exist; without the latter `Product.products(for:)` returned empty
  in every build and the purchase path had never been exercisable. Pinned by `premium.test.sql`
  (plan 55) and `PremiumSubscriptionTests`. **Still open in this area:** the resource gates
  themselves (digest predicate, attachment caps, chat quota tiering) — the foundation is in, no
  quota has changed yet — plus the local-vs-server entitlement downgrade and the dead
  `weeklyDigestEnabled` toggle.
- **The RLS canary was passing vacuously for all three premium tables.** They were absent from
  the explicit table list in `rls_policies.test.sql`; added, 21 → 24 tables, plan 37 → 44.
- **`public.profiles` had no explicit grant.** It was the only policy-bearing table relying on
  Supabase default privileges, which a freshly provisioned database does not apply — a staging
  project built from these migrations alone would have denied every profile read. Found by the
  first CI run of the database job, which had never executed before.
- **ChatAttachment could not decode the server's attachment metadata.** `ChatAttachment` used
  synthesized `Codable` (camelCase keys, required `id`) while `begin_nina_chat_run` writes
  `{kind, filename, mime_type, byte_count}`. One photographed boleto therefore threw inside
  `loadNinaState`, failed the whole household snapshot, and — because a cached home is correctly
  discarded on a failed membership check — locked the user out of their own home until the 30-day
  retention sweep. `ChatAttachment` and `NinaProposalPayload` now have hand-written `init(from:)`
  with snake_case keys and defaults, per the repo's model convention. Pinned by
  `RemoteDecodingTests`.
- **The workload chart was hardcoded with the founder's family.** `WorkloadBars` literally read
  "Mirna 82 / Heitor 8" on Hoje and again under any Casa insight whose title contained "Carga";
  `PreviewData.insights` asserted the matching "82 x 8"; `family_members.task_count` is a column
  nothing writes, so every member card read 0. Replaced by `HouseholdWorkload`, computed from the
  household's own open committed tasks, with guardrails: seeds excluded, "Casa" counted separately
  and never attributed to a person, and no split drawn at all below 6 assigned tasks across at
  least 2 people. Pinned by `HouseholdWorkloadTests`.
- **Overdue tasks vanished from Hoje.** `isDue(on:)` was same-day only, so a boleto due yesterday
  disappeared and "Atrasadas" read 0. `displayDate(relativeTo:)` now resolves a recurring task's
  current occurrence — so the screen and the notification scheduler agree — and the new
  `belongsOnAgenda(for:)` keeps anything due today or earlier on the list. Pinned by
  `TaskAgendaTests`.
- **Every phone received every task's reminder.** The scheduler had no owner predicate, so another
  adult's chores buzzed your phone and evicted your own reminders from the 60-request tail.
  `synchronize(tasks:familyID:viewerName:)` now schedules only your tasks plus unowned "Casa"
  tasks, failing open when the viewer is unknown. Pinned by `NotificationTargetingTests`.
- **Quiet hours silently moved a time the user had chosen.** A 06:30 medicine reminder was
  delivered at 07:00 while the card kept showing 06:30. Quiet hours now silences the alert
  (`sound = nil`, `.passive`) at the chosen time instead of moving it.
- **Tapping anywhere on a Tarefas card completed the task**, with no undo, and on a recurring task
  irreversibly rolled the due date forward. Card tap now opens the editor; the circle completes.
- **Shopping items could never be deleted.** Added `deleteShoppingItem` through the backend
  protocol, store and editor, plus bulk "Limpar comprados", a pending/bought split, an empty state,
  a real member picker for Responsável, and "Adicionar e continuar" for building a list without
  reopening the sheet per item.
- **No search, filter or completed-task separation in Tarefas.** Added `.searchable` over title,
  responsável and categoria, a Todas / Minhas / Atrasadas / Sementes filter bar with live counts,
  overdue-first sorting, and a collapsed "concluídas" disclosure.
- **App Store 3.1.2 and LGPD links were absent.** The paywall now carries renewal terms, Termos de
  uso, Privacidade and Gerenciar assinatura; Ajustes carries the privacy policy, terms and support;
  the AI consent card links the policy and states that messages and attachments are processed by a
  model provider outside Brazil.

### Traps and surprises

- The wishlist is mostly bloat, and the real gaps are more basic than any of it. Before aisles, stores, pantry staples, prices or units, this feature is missing DELETE. There is no `deleteShoppingItem` anywhere in the repo — not in the protocol (RemoteHomeBackend.swift:120-121), not in AppStore, not in the sheet, not as a swipe action. Every item a family ever adds is permanent. That single omission dominates every wishlist item.
- Which wishlist items I would deliberately NOT build. (1) Price/budget per item — Brazilian supermarket prices move weekly and per store; hand-entered prices decay instantly, and it converts a relief product into a spreadsheet, plus it would push household financial detail through the AI pipeline for marginal payoff. (2) Aisles/corredores — aisle layout is per-store and per-city, so there is no taxonomy to ship; it becomes manual categorisation, i.e. exactly the mental load Nina exists to absorb. The existing TaskCategory tones (food/pet/health) already give free visual grouping. (3) Structured quantity units — `amount` is free text today and the app's own demo data ("1 botijão", "3 kg", "5 dias") shows why that is right; a kg/un/L picker adds taps and cannot express "5 dias de fruta". (4) Multi-store lists — a whole extra entity and filter dimension for a household that does one supermarket run plus an occasional farmácia; the title field absorbs it today. (5) Pantry-staples inventory — a second data model (quantity on hand, depletion rate) nobody maintains past week two. (6) "Live shared shopping mode" as a distinct screen — realtime replication ALREADY works (202606140001:21,31 plus RemoteHomeBackend.swift:747-795); a "modo mercado" surface would add a screen without adding a capability. What is actually missing is per-item concurrency and a cheaper refresh, which is gap #6.
- My recommended minimal set, in order: inline rapid entry (#1), delete + "limpar comprados" (#2), pending/bought split with an empty state (#3), owner as a real member with MemberTone so shopping counts toward the fairness signal (#4). Those four are almost entirely client-side and turn the feature from a demo into something a family can use every Saturday. Only then the multi-item AI proposal (#5), which is the one genuinely differentiated capability.
- `shopping_items.owner_member_id` (202606020001_initial_nina_schema.sql:105) is a fully declared FK to family_members that no code in the entire repo — Swift, SQL, or TypeScript — ever writes or reads. A naive implementer will assume shopping owners are already typed members. They are not; `owner_label` free text is the only thing that exists.

- The AI shopping path is more complete than CLAUDE.md's "flagship AI feature ships off" note implies, and also more constrained. - Shopping is entirely absent from local notifications. `Nina/LocalNotifications.swift` contains no shopping reference at all, so "leite acabou" never produces a reminder even though the same household's tasks do. Whether that is right is a product call, but it is a silent asymmetry.

### Traps and surprises

- HOUSE STYLE, verbatim, so a new migration can be written without re-reading the files. Filename `YYYYMMDDNNNN_snake_case.sql`, applied in filename order — on-disk mtimes do NOT match name order (202606190002 has mtime Jul 29, 202606190003 has Jul 24). Recent migrations wrap the whole file in `begin;` / `commit;`. Tables: `create table public.x (...)` with `id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid()`, `created_at timestamptz not null default now()`, `updated_at timestamptz not null default now()`; then `create trigger x_set_updated_at before update on public.x for each row execute function public.set_updated_at();`; then `alter table public.x enable row level security; revoke all on table public.x from public, anon, authenticated;` and only the privileges actually needed. Functions: `... security definer set search_path = pg_catalog, public, auth` (older files use `public, auth`; new work uses the pg_catalog-first form), ALWAYS followed by `revoke all on function public.f(argtypes) from public, anon, authenticated, service_role;` plus exactly one `grant execute ... to <one role>`. Postgres grants EXECUTE TO PUBLIC on every new function, and a signature change creates a NEW function with fresh PUBLIC rights — re-run the revoke/grant block with the exact new argument list every time (202607290002:252-263 and :457-483 `drop function if exists` the 10-arg update_family_member and re-grant it with the full type list spelled out across lines).
- ERROR CODES ARE API SURFACE, matched by both Swift and pgTAP: 28000 not_authenticated · 42501 denied (family_access_denied, family_management_denied, permission_role_denied, nina_proposal_access_denied) · 22023 invalid argument · 23514 limit reached (family_member_limit_reached, invite_use_limit_reached) · P0001 business rule (invalid_invite_code, nina_rate_limited, nina_budget_exceeded) · P0002 not found. Messages are stable snake_case ENGLISH; the pt-BR copy lives in the Swift view/store layer, never in the exception. Closed value sets are always `text` + a named CHECK altered via `drop constraint if exists` / `add constraint` so migrations re-run — there is not one Postgres enum in the schema.
- LOCK ORDER IS GLOBAL AND NON-NEGOTIABLE: read only enough state to pick the lock, then `perform pg_catalog.pg_advisory_xact_lock(pg_catalog.hashtextextended(family_id::text, 0));`, then `select ... for update`, then RE-VERIFY that family_id still matches the id you locked on (202607290002:22-50 is canonical). The re-verify is not cosmetic — the row could have moved families between the two reads. Every mutation RPC returns `public.get_current_home_context()` so the client replaces state wholesale rather than patching. Realtime needs both `replica identity full` and a DO block adding the table to supabase_realtime that swallows `duplicate_object`.
- `public.family_snapshots` is a completely dead table. `authenticated` was revoked insert/update/delete (202606100005:21-30) and no RPC, Edge Function or client ever writes it — so `get_current_home_context()` unconditionally returns `"snapshot": null` (202606190002:191-194, :299). The Swift client ignores the field entirely (Nina/RemoteHomeBackend.swift:969 hardcodes `snapshot: nil`) and rebuilds the snapshot from individual table reads at :817. Do not build anything on top of it assuming it holds state.
- The RLS canary's explicit list is 21 names and does NOT include the three tables from 202606160001: premium_subscriptions, premium_subscription_transactions, app_store_server_notifications. All three do have RLS enabled (202606160001:96-98), so this is not a live hole — but per CLAUDE.md §10 they should be listed, and today a policy regression on the subscription tables would pass the canary silently. Adding any table means editing three things in supabase/tests/database/rls_policies.test.sql: the array at :173-195, the `21` literal at :197, and `plan(37)` at :5.
- `authenticated` still holds full INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE on public.household_insights (202606100005:11-19), a table whose rows are now written exclusively by the service_role-only complete_nina_insight_run. This directly contradicts the AI-writes/client-reads posture enforced everywhere else — nina_proposals and nina_threads are select-only, and memory_items has no INSERT grant at all.
- `memory_items` deliberately has NO insert grant for authenticated — only `select, update, delete` (202606150001:298). Memories can be created only by resolve_nina_proposal. A "let the user write their own memory" feature therefore needs a new SECURITY DEFINER RPC, not a grant; adding `insert` would silently bypass the confirm-before-persist invariant that the whole trust proposition rests on.
- `resolve_nina_proposal` still accepts `kind = 'reminder'` and the nina_proposals_kind_check still lists it (202606150001:165), even though the reminders table was dropped. The post-unification version routes 'reminder' into `tasks` with a synthetic category_snapshot carrying symbolName and `tone: 'amber'` (202606190003:185-223). Anyone "cleaning up" the dead reminder kind breaks acceptance of in-flight proposals.
- Neither `tasks` nor `shopping_items` has an index supporting the query the app actually runs on every home refresh — `where family_id = $1 order by created_at desc` (Nina/RemoteHomeBackend.swift:863-885). All three tasks indexes are partial and serve other predicates: tasks_family_due_at_idx (due_at not null and not is_done), tasks_family_snoozed_until_idx, tasks_family_open_seeds_idx. The primary list is a seq scan plus sort. Same for household_insights and memory_items.
- `family_members.tone` has NO check constraint despite being a closed set (mint|coral|sky|amber|lavender, Nina/Models.swift:30-38) and despite the repo's own rule of text + named CHECK for closed sets. update_family_member passes it straight through untrimmed and unvalidated (202607290002:365). A bad value silently falls back to `.mint` via decodeIfPresent, so nothing fails loudly.
- `add_unclaimed_family_member`'s household_role default silently changed from 'adult' (202606100003:516) to 'child' (202606190002:676) when the signature grew from 6 to 9 parameters. Only the 9-arg version is granted; the 6-arg one was drop-function-if-exists'd. Any caller relying on the positional default now gets a different role.
- `get_current_nina_state` returns the ENTIRE thread with no LIMIT and no pagination (202606150001:784-822), with proposals sub-aggregated per message, and it is loaded on every home refresh. The only bound is the 30-day chat retention against a 30-request-per-hour user quota.

- The honest metric already exists server-side and is only reachable through the chatbot. `get_workload_summary` (supabase/functions/nina-chat/index.ts:303-324) computes real open/completed/urgent counts per owner from live tasks — but it is an OpenAI tool declaration, not an RPC, so the iOS app cannot call it. Meanwhile the UI that is *about* workload uses literals. The gap is plumbing, not computation.
- CLAUDE.md §13 says the weekly digest "runs for every family with no entitlement predicate". The entitlement half is right, but it is also capped at 25 families per day globally (`candidates.slice(0, 25)`, supabase/functions/nina-maintenance/index.ts:160) with no ordering, no cursor, and no fairness rotation. Past 25 eligible families, an arbitrary subset gets insights every day and the rest silently never do — so "insights" is not a feature every paying family will actually receive.
- `public.tasks` has no `completed_at` column, and the client never selects `created_at` or `created_by` (Nina/RemoteHomeBackend.swift:926). Every string in the app that promises a time window is therefore uncomputable as written: PreviewData's "nos últimos 30 dias" (AppStore.swift:2455), the member detail row "Tarefas nos últimos 30 dias" (Components.swift:803), and the marketing benefit copy. Even the server proxies completion with `updated_at >= now() - 7 days and is_done`, which recounts a task completed a month ago if anyone edits it. Anyone implementing "tasks completed this week" honestly needs a migration first.
- The landing page and the app disagree about what the feature even is. web/src/pages/index.astro:225-244 shows three bars as percentages (Mirna 82%, Heitor 48%, Casa 64% — summing to 194%, so they are not shares of anything), while Nina/TodayView.swift:628-629 shows two bars as raw counts (82, 8). A live implementation has to pick one; raw counts are the safer choice, since percentages of a whole are exactly what turns a portrait into a scoreboard.
- `resetActivityState()` is `apply(.preview)` and fires from fourteen call sites including the `.unavailable` branch and a cache-decode failure (AppStore.swift:2006). A naive implementer will gate the live metric on `store.tasks.isEmpty` or on `homeAccessState == .authorized`; both are defeated, because the preview seed leaves seven owned tasks and the demo family group in place. Only an explicit provenance flag set at the two `apply(state)` success sites (AppStore.swift:443, :541) is a reliable gate.
- The weekly insight's numbers are model-authored free text. `insightResponseSchema` (supabase/functions/_shared/nina-ai.ts:156-181) types `metric` and `message` as bare strings, and the deterministic metrics are handed to the model only as a JSON blob in the prompt. The no-blame constraint that CLAUDE.md calls the thing preventing a fairness chart from becoming a weapon is enforced by prompt text alone, with no post-generation validation of either the tone or the arithmetic.

### Traps and surprises

- CLAUDE.md §1 is stale in the way the brief says (the tree is committed and clean), but its architectural claims for this area hold up under source inspection — the member-management RPCs, the lock ordering, and the permission matrix are all genuinely implemented and genuinely tested (27 pgTAP assertions in supabase/tests/database/member_management.test.sql). This area is one of the more finished parts of the repo; the wishlist framing of 'edit members, remove people, change roles' as work-to-do is wrong. What is missing is almost entirely on the read side — surfacing, notifying, and reading back data that is already correctly stored.
- Child and pet profiles ARE first-class in the database and in the editor (dedicated birth_date / pet_species / pet_breed columns, a role-conditional editor section, pgTAP coverage) — but they are second-class everywhere the user actually looks. The member card, the avatar, the AI context and the notification scheduler all ignore those columns. A naive implementer told 'make child/pet profiles first-class' would add columns that already exist and duplicate the editor that already works, while leaving the actual defect untouched.
- Nina/TodayView.swift:625-632 hardcodes two real private individuals' names (Mirna, Heitor) with fabricated workload numbers, and TodayView.swift:14 renders that card unconditionally to every user on the Hoje tab. It reads as demo data that was never wired up. Given that the same names appear in PreviewData.insights, whoever fixes this should also check that CLAUDE.md's 'Demo data is the empty state' trap has not leaked into other surfaces the same way.
- family_join_requests is `revoke all from public, anon, authenticated` with RLS on and zero policies (202606190002:103-107) — deliberately reachable only through RPCs. That is correct security design, and it is also exactly why the obvious fix for the missing join notification (publish the table to supabase_realtime) cannot work: realtime evaluates RLS as the subscriber, and the subscriber has no read path. The signal has to ride a table clients already read.
- Tasks are owned by a free-text `owner_label` (202606020001:88), not by a family_members foreign key, and update_family_member deliberately refuses to rename a claimed member (202606190002:807-810) because the name is owned by profiles.display_name. The consequence is invisible until you look for it: an adult who renames themselves in ProfileEditorView silently splits their own workload history into two buckets, and any per-member task count computed by name join will under-report them. Any workload or redistribution work must decide whether to fix the join key or accept the drift.
- MemberDetailContent (Components.swift:779), MemberDetailSheet (Sheets.swift:2563) and MemberRouteDetail (AppRootView.swift:1189) are all dead — RouterPath.navigate(to:) at AppRootView.swift:99 has zero call sites anywhere in the repo. A read-only member view was written and then orphaned when every card was pointed at the editor sheet instead. This is the single cheapest fix in the area: the component to show non-admins already exists and just needs to be routed to.
- Ownership succession exists, but only as a side effect of account deletion (202608020002:106-119 promotes the longest-tenured remaining adult). There is no deliberate transfer path, and no one — not even an admin — can remove or replace an owner. So the documented invariant 'owner can be neither granted nor revoked via any RPC' is exactly true, and its practical consequence is that an abandoned owner account permanently freezes the household.

### Traps and surprises

- CLAUDE.md's trap "nothing wipes protected local data on plain sign-out" is literally true, but its severity is lower than it reads: activateHomeContext (AppStore.swift:400-476) never applies a cached home without live server confirmation, so a signed-out or removed member cannot read that data through the UI. The real exposure is indefinite at-rest retention with no user-facing purge, not in-app leakage. Do not "fix" it by adding a cache fallback.
- The onboarding tutorial runs BEFORE the home exists — AppRootView.entryPhase evaluates .tutorial (AppRootView.swift:284) before .homeSetup (:299). Every wishlist onboarding step (who lives here, first task, first invite) writes household data, and enqueueRemoteMutation silently no-ops with no active home. A naive implementer will add slides to OnboardingTutorialView and ship a flow whose writes vanish with no error.
- AI consent has no server counterpart at all. There is no consent table in any of the 24 migrations (only the marketing waitlist has consent columns), nina-chat has zero occurrences of "consent", and get_nina_weekly_candidates has no consent predicate. The consent card is an honest UI over an unenforced promise.
- private.nina_weekly_metrics builds open_tasks_by_owner as a JSON map of real member display names to open-task counts and sends it to OpenAI weekly. This is the exact "Sinal de sobrecarga" fairness data, leaving Brazil for a US provider, for every family with ≥5 events, regardless of consent.
- Notification permission is already requested contextually — in the task editor at the moment a dated task is created (Sheets.swift:1915-1955) and again in the notification settings screen. That is better iOS practice than the wishlist's "choose notification preference during onboarding", so that wishlist item is arguably already satisfied better than asked. The quiet-hour schedule editor also already exists and works (Sheets.swift:943-967).
- There is no "Sair da casa" anywhere, and remove_family_member explicitly denies self-removal (202607290002_join_concurrency_hardening.sql:427-434). For a product built around two-adult households, the separation scenario has no exit short of account deletion.
- OperationalDiagnostics.shared.start() runs unconditionally at every launch (NinaApp.swift:15), archives up to 12 MetricKit payloads / 8 MB into Application Support, is never surfaced in settings, is not listed in the privacy page's §9 controls, is not in the JSON export, and is not removed by account deletion. ### Traps and surprises

- CLAUDE.md's framing implies proposals are ephemeral chat decoration. They are not — they are fully persisted and fully re-hydrated. The gap is purely presentational: `get_current_nina_state` already returns every proposal on every home load (supabase/migrations/202606150001_nina_ai_v2.sql:794-814), so `AppStore.messages` in memory already contains the complete pending set. A naive implementer would write a new RPC and a new migration for the Inbox; none is needed. A one-line derived property over `messages.flatMap(\.proposals)` is the whole data layer.
- Proposals are reachable ONLY through `assistant_message_id`. The `get_current_nina_state` subquery joins `where proposals.assistant_message_id = messages.id`, but the FK is `on delete set null` (supabase/migrations/202606150001_nina_ai_v2.sql:164). Any future change that prunes chat messages more aggressively than the current 30-day `retained_until` would orphan live pending proposals into invisibility while leaving them resolvable by id. An Inbox that queries the table directly is structurally more robust than one derived from messages — that is a real argument for the direct query despite the derived property being cheaper today.
- `chat_messages.suggestion` is never written for v2 turns. `complete_nina_chat_run` (supabase/migrations/202606150001_nina_ai_v2.sql:558-577) omits the column, and the legacy suggestion the Edge Function synthesises via `attachLegacySuggestion` exists only in the live HTTP response. So with NINA_AI_V2_ENABLED = NO, every actionable card in the app has a lifetime of one screen appearance — switch tabs and it is gone forever. CLAUDE.md says "she can talk; she cannot organize"; the sharper truth is that she appears to organize for about thirty seconds.
- `applySuggestion` (Nina/AppStore.swift:1171-1176) writes its confirmation message through `createChatMessage`, and `ChatMessageInsertRow` (Nina/RemoteHomeBackend.swift:1470-1500) has no `thread_id`. `get_current_nina_state` filters `where messages.thread_id = target_thread_id`. Those rows are written to the database and then never read by anything, on any surface. Same for the entire `shouldPersistLegacyTurn` branch at Nina/AppStore.swift:1118-1126.
- `resolve_nina_proposal` still contains a `when 'reminder'` branch (supabase/migrations/202606190003_unify_tasks_and_reminders.sql:186), but `reminders` was dropped in that same migration (line 403). The branch survives correctly because it was rewritten to insert into `tasks` with a synthetic amber `category_snapshot` — but the earlier definition in 202606150001 at lines 969-990 still inserts into `public.reminders`, so anyone reading migrations in directory order rather than applying them will read a version that references a dropped table. Trust the later file.
- In Release builds the local snapshot cache is write-only. `cacheAppSnapshotLocally` runs on every mutation, but `loadActivityState` is called from exactly one place — `activateLocalHomeContext`, which is DEBUG/no-backend only (Nina/AppStore.swift:1957-1962). Every chat message, proposal and attachment thumbnail is being encrypted to disk under `ProtectedLocalDataStore` and never read back. That is dead I/O on the hot path and, given the attachment pipeline carries boletos and prescriptions (CLAUDE.md §13), a data-at-rest footprint with no corresponding benefit.
- The `resolve()` call in `NinaProposalCard` (Nina/NinaChatView.swift:1094-1104) always sends `editedPayload` on accept, whether or not the user ever opened the editor. It is a no-op today because the drafts are seeded from the payload in `init` — but it means the server's `payload || edited_payload` merge is always exercised, so any future field added to `NinaProposalPayload` without a matching draft would be silently overwritten with its Swift default on every accept.
- `open_tasks_by_owner` in `private.nina_weekly_metrics` groups by trimmed `owner_label` free text (supabase/migrations/202606190003_unify_tasks_and_reminders.sql:341-355), and `resolve_nina_proposal` never populates `tasks.owner_member_id`. The "Sinal de sobrecarga" number — the feature the whole product is quietly built around — is computed from strings the model wrote and the user can retype in a proposal card.

### Traps and surprises

- The site has ZERO App Store or download affordance of any kind. Grepping web/src and web/public case-insensitively for 'app store|baixar|download|testflight|apple_id' returns exactly one hit — an unrelated ph:download-simple-fill icon on the privacy page. Every CTA on every page, including the invite page's primary action, routes either to the waitlist dialog or to a nina:// custom scheme. A naive implementer would assume a badge just needs a URL; the real work is that there is no 'install' state in the information architecture at all.
- Apple's official App Store badge is served from Apple's CDN and img-src 'self' data: blocks it outright. Any badge must be a checked-in asset under web/public/images/. This is exactly the class of failure CLAUDE.md warns about: astro check passes, the build passes, and the image is blank only in production.
- Do NOT reach for FAQPage JSON-LD as the SEO fix. <script type="application/ld+json"> is still an inline <script> element, and the repo rule ('Never add an inline <script> or <style>') is absolute precisely because CSP failures here are runtime-only and untestable by astro check. Browser behaviour for non-JS script types under a strict script-src is not uniform enough to gamble a production regression on.
- legal.ts's module-scope import.meta.env read is a pattern, not an accident — and any new build-time config module (an App Store ID, a price string, a feature flag) inherits both its virtues (placeholder rejection, one source of truth, testable in isolation) and its footgun (changing a Cloudflare variable does nothing until the site is rebuilt AND redeployed). Any such new file must also be added by name to deno.json's check, format:web and lint:web task lists, because deno.json enumerates individual files, not directories — otherwise it is silently never type-checked, linted or formatted and CI stays green.
- run_waitlist_retention (supabase/migrations/202607290001_web_waitlist.sql:234-236) hard-deletes every status='unsubscribed' row on the daily cron, while register_waitlist_signup:186 sets status='subscribed' on conflict. Erasing the PII on withdrawal is LGPD-correct, but it means there is no durable suppression list, so the moment a real email provider is introduced an import or an accidental re-submit will silently resurrect someone who opted out. Fix this in the same change as the sender, not after.
- web/AGENTS.md calls this directory a 'Prototype' and instructs agents to use a 'Product Design plugin' — it directly contradicts the production hardening (Worker security headers, non-enumerating responses, preflight gates, 24 Deno tests) sitting in the same directory. Treat CLAUDE.md section 8 as authoritative for web/, not AGENTS.md.
- web/src/pages/invite/ and web/src/pages/invite-shell/ exist as EMPTY leftover directories. They are harmless today, but dropping an index.astro into src/pages/invite/ would build a real /invite/ asset that the Worker's run_worker_first rewrite would shadow anyway — a confusing dead route. Delete them or leave them; do not build into them.
- join/index.astro:11 sets canonical="/invite/", a path that only ever exists as a rewrite source and never as a real page, so og:url on every shared invite resolves to a codeless https://ninai.app/invite/. Harmless because the page is noindex and the pasted link is what actually opens, but it means the invite page's social metadata is intentionally generic — which is correct (a server-rendered family name would leak the household name to every link-preview crawler) and should be preserved deliberately rather than 'fixed'.
- The strongest guarantees on this surface are the ones easiest to break by accident while adding pages: the byte-identical CSP in web/public/_headers:10 and web/src/worker.ts:30, and run_worker_first = ["/api/*", "/invite/*"] in wrangler.toml:8. Nothing in the test suite or the preflight compares the two CSP strings to each other, and nothing at all guards run_worker_first — removing it breaks /api/* and the invite rewrite silently in production only.
